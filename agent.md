## 4. Newly Integrated Projects (Full Technical Breakdowns)

### New Project A: Star Wars BB-8 Replica Droid
* **Project Paradigm:** A fully functional, radio-controlled, and autonomous-capable replica of the BB-8 droid, featuring a 16.125-inch outer diameter hollow body and an active, stabilized floating head mechanism.
* **System Architecture (Pendulum Chassis):**
    * *Locomotion Mechanism:* Rejected simple internal wheel tracks ("hamster-wheel drive") in favor of a central horizontal 0.5-inch diameter aluminum shaft fixed directly to paddles on the sphere's poles. A high-torque 12V DC motor drives the shell through a custom bevel gear linkage.
    * *Stability & Steering:* A 1.8 kg custom-designed 3D-printed flywheel acts as a pendulum counterweight suspended from bearings on the central shaft. Spinning this counterweight creates an inertial reaction torque that causes the sphere to pitch and yaw smoothly, lowering the center of gravity while driving realistic, fluid motion.
    * *Chassis Carrier:* A 3D-printed PLA carrier platform houses the entire electronics bay, battery, and motors, remaining vertically stabilized by gravity as the sphere rotates around it.
* **Head Stabilization & Interface:**
    * *Magnetic Linkage:* The head (carved from styrofoam blocks using guided profile tracing and hollowed for electronics) features a 3D-printed base housing 3 high-grade Neodymium magnets. These link with a matching set of Neodymium magnets mounted on a 3D-printed vertical shaft that extends up from the swinging internal pendulum chassis.
    * *Friction Mitigation:* To let the sphere rotate while the head remains upright, the head base integrates three 5/8-inch omnidirectional ball bearing casters. These roll smoothly over the exterior track of the sphere, counteracting the combined normal and downward magnetic force.
* **Electrical & Control Systems:**
    * *Dual Microcontroller Logic:* Powered by a centralized 11.1V 3S LiPo battery (2200mAh) feeding a Cytron MDD10A dual motor driver. An ESP32-S2 serves as the main body controller, running software acceleration/deceleration ramps to smooth out torque spikes. An independent ESP32-C3 sits in the head, executing a continuous capture loop via an Arducam and streaming a live MJPEG video feed over a local WiFi network to an HTML command dashboard.
* **Engineering Validation & Problem Solving:**
    * *Structural Finite Element Verification:* Analyzed the 0.5-inch aluminum rod to ensure zero bending under the 5.5 kg total system mass. Bending stress registered at 14.86 MPa (Safety Factor of 18.6 vs. 276 MPa yield strength) with a deflection of just 0.47 mm, keeping the custom bevel gears perfectly aligned.
    * *Flywheel Physics Iteration:* The initial 1.0 kg counterweight failed to overcome the sphere's high static friction ($5.0	ext{ Nm}$ with a friction coefficient of 0.45). By optimizing the interior geometry to angle the weights and printing the flywheel shell at 100% infill, mass was increased to 1.75 kg at a 4-inch radius, providing $1.74	ext{ Nm}$ of torque to reliably control rotation.
    * *Real-World Adaptation:* Addressed ABS thermal warping from Addlab Fortus prints through meticulous post-print sanding. Solved caster wheel binding caused by surface debris by implementing an inline, low-friction track caster at the bottom of the flywheel to distribute the physical load.
    * *Control Paradigm Pivot:* When internal mechanical rotation stressed direct wire leads and disrupted RC PWM signals, the control architecture was successfully pivoted to an onboard HTML web server hosted directly on the ESP32-S2 over WiFi, establishing flawless operation.

        * **Layer 1 (Bottom):** Dedicated I2C distance sensor interface. Isolates signal paths and provides dedicated control lines.
        * **Layer 2 (Center - Command Center):** Directly mounts the ESP32-S2 microcontroller, the Cytron MDD10A motor driver, and an LM2596 buck converter. Keeps high-current motor loops extremely short to prevent voltage drops and brownout resets.

    * *State Machine Logic:* Governed by an onboard Finite State Machine (FSM) managing four distinct modes: Manual Teleoperation, Point-to-Point Navigation, Obstacle Avoidance, and Wall Following.
    * *Autonomous Navigation Layer:* Uses a robust "turn-then-drive" pathfinding algorithm with a Proportional (P) controller to eliminate wheel slip from complex curves. Implements proportional deceleration when within 150mm of target coordinates.

        2.  *Static Mapping:* An internal lookup table holding coordinate boundaries for all permanent obstacles. The system checks a projection vector 150mm ahead; if a collision is detected, it calculates an automated detour path.
        3.  *Dynamic Avoidance:* Three VL53L0X Time-of-Flight (ToF) distance sensors act as a virtual bumper system. Detections closer than 150mm abort the linear path and trigger an active wall-following sub-routine.
        4.  *Global Calibration:* Dual top-mounted Vive photodiodes map raw signal ticks in a central zone. To handle signal drops, data is processed offline via a calibrated bilinear coordinate transform mapping coefficients ($a_0-a_3, b_0-b_3$) into the internal inch-based dead-reckoning coordinate system.

* **Technical Problem Solving (The I2C Lockup Victory):**
    * *The Bug:* Sudden current draw from the high-torque gearmotors during startup caused brief dips on the primary power rail. This voltage drop caused the sensitive ToF sensors to reset, pulling the I2C SDA data line hard to ground and freezing the ESP32-S2 microcontroller, leading to runaway driving.
    * *The Fix:* After software watchdogs failed to clear the electrical lock, the power layout was updated. The ToF sensors were re-routed from the 5V rail to an isolated, highly regulated 3.3V rail on Layer 1, bypassing the sensors' internal regulators and entirely eliminating motor-induced brownouts.
